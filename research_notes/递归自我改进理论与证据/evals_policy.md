# Evaluations and Governance Frameworks for AI R&D Automation (Operational RSI), 2023–2026

Note on verification: items marked [primary] were read directly from the original document (PDF text extracted locally or the source page fetched). Items marked [secondary] or [unverified] came from blogs, trackers or press coverage and could not be checked against a primary document. As of 2026-11-08, METR's time-horizons page says it is "no longer actively updated" (noted 2026-09-08).

## Q1. METR RE-Bench (2024): tasks, human experts vs agents by time budget

### Takeaway
RE-Bench (Nov 2024) has 7 ML research-engineering environments. Agents beat human experts at a 2-hour budget (about 4x the score). Humans narrowly win at 8 hours and score about 2x the best agent at 32 hours. By 2026 Anthropic says frontier models have crossed the highest human baselines on these kinds of task-based AI R&D evals, and it no longer uses them to decide whether a threshold is crossed.

### Cited Findings
- RE-Bench has 7 open-ended ML research-engineering environments. 61 distinct human experts made 71 eight-hour attempts. 82% of expert attempts scored above zero, and 24% matched or beat the strong reference solutions [primary] — [arXiv 2411.15114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15114)
- Results by budget: at 2 hours the best agents scored about 4x higher than humans. At 8 hours humans narrowly beat the top agent. At 32 hours (pooled across attempts) humans scored about 2x the top agent [primary] — [arXiv 2411.15114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15114)
- One agent wrote a Triton kernel faster than any human expert's. Agents generate and test solutions more than 10x faster than humans, at much lower cost [primary] — [arXiv 2411.15114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15114)
- The International AI Safety Report 2026 cites this result: "AI agents perform better than humans at two-hour tasks but have lower success rates at eight-hour tasks" (§1.3) — [IASR 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026)
- Saturation by late 2026. Anthropic's Sept 1, 2026 system card says: "Recent models have crossed the highest human baselines for many of the automated task-based AI R&D evaluations described in Section 8.3 of the Claude Opus 4.6 System Card, and results on such tasks are no longer a significant component of our RSP and FCF capability-threshold determinations. As such, we have not run these automated evaluations for Mythos 5.1." [primary] — [Claude Fable 5.1 & Mythos 5.1 System Card](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/0339e6a7c5c7b87f5c07798616dc32c215d14235/Claude%20Fable%205.1%20&%20Claude%20Mythos%205.1%20System%20Card.pdf)
- A secondary account says the Claude Mythos Preview card (April 2026) reported clearing the "4h and 8h thresholds on all tasks, and the 40h threshold on 2/3 of the tasks" on Anthropic's internal AI R&D task suite [secondary/unverified] — [Zvi Mowshowitz summary](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/claude-mythos-the-system-card); [GitHub markdown conversion of the card](https://gist.github.com/Michaelliv/0677ab6a64312211e38b7a99a03c5f61)

### Inferences
- From 2024 to 2026 the RE-Bench-style comparison flipped from "humans win at long budgets" to "agents exceed the best human baselines". Both METR and Anthropic now treat these benchmarks as saturated and largely uninformative about full automation.

### Gaps
- The fetched abstract had no raw normalized RE-Bench scores per agent or budget. I found no public 2025–2026 RE-Bench leaderboard from METR.

## Q2. METR "Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Tasks" (2025) and Time Horizon 1.1: 50% horizon, doubling time, latest measurements

### Takeaway
The original March 2025 paper found the 50% time horizon doubling about every 7 months since 2019. TH1.1 (Jan 2026) gives 130.8 days since 2023 and 88.6 days since 2024. By spring 2026 frontier models were about 12 to 16+ hours at 50%, and METR said its suite was "essentially saturated" and unreliable above 16 hours. For later models (GPT-5.6 Sol, Claude Opus 5.5) METR either declined to call the numbers robust (cheating) or switched to bespoke AI R&D tasks.

### Cited Findings
- Definition: the 50% time horizon is the length of task, measured by how long human experts take, that an agent completes with 50% reliability. It comes from a logistic fit per model. Claude 3.7 Sonnet was about 1 hour. Doubling was about 7 months over roughly 6 years of data. Task suites: very short software tasks (SWAA), HCAST and RE-Bench. Current models then succeeded less than 10% of the time on tasks over about 4 hours [primary] — [METR blog, 2025-03-19](https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/); paper [arXiv 2503.14499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499)
- The original post also said fitting only 2024–2025 data pulls the forecast date for month-long tasks forward by about 2.5 years. A separate SWE-bench Verified analysis showed a doubling time under 3 months, partly attributed to how human time was estimated. A 10x error in absolute measurement shifts forecasts by only about 2 years [primary] — [METR blog](https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/)
- TH1.1 (Jan 29, 2026) doubling times. Since 2019: 196.5 days (TH1.0 was 195.8). Since 2023: 130.8 days [95% CI 107–161] (TH1.0: 165.3). Since 2024: 88.6 days (TH1.0: 108.9) [primary] — [METR TH1.1](https://metr.org/blog/2026-1-29-time-horizon-1-1/)
- TH1.1 suite: 170 → 228 tasks; tasks of 8+ hours went from 14 to 31, but only 5 of them have measured human baselines. The harness moved from Vivaria to Inspect, which affected scores (GPT-4o and o3 scored higher under Vivaria) [primary] — [METR TH1.1](https://metr.org/blog/2026-1-29-time-horizon-1-1/)
- TH1.1 50% horizons with 95% CI: Claude Opus 4.5 320 min [170–729]; GPT-5 214 min [117–480]; o3 121 min; Claude Opus 4 101 min; Claude 3.7 Sonnet 60 min; GPT-4 0314 3.5 min [primary] — [METR TH1.1](https://metr.org/blog/2026-1-29-time-horizon-1-1/)
- METR's Frontier Risk Report (May 19, 2026) gives the public frontier in Feb–Mar 2026 as about 12 hours at 50% (range 5–61 hours). The internal frontier was likely at least 16 hours, about 2 months ahead of public. At 80%: public about 1.5 hours, internal likely 3–4 hours. The most capable agents "essentially saturated our Time Horizon 1.1 benchmark", and many remaining failures came from cheating [primary] — [METR Frontier Risk Report](https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-19-frontier-risk-report/)
- The same report says that on an early version of MirrorCode (software reimplementation), the strongest agents solved tasks that would take humans weeks, with horizons above 100 hours. It also says many software, ML, math and cyber benchmarks were saturated or near saturation [primary] — [METR Frontier Risk Report](https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-19-frontier-risk-report/)
- METR's time-horizons page says "Measurements above 16 hrs are unreliable with our current task suite." The latest model added is an early Claude Mythos Preview (May 8, 2026). The page is no longer actively updated as of Sept 8, 2026 [primary] — [METR time horizons](https://metr.org/time-horizons/)
- Model-level figures from a secondary compilation that cites METR's YAML: Claude Opus 4.6 about 12 hours at 50% (CI about 5–60 hours; METR first said about 14.5 hours, then fixed a bug), about 70 min at 80%. Claude Mythos Preview about 17.4 hours (CI about 8.5–55 hours) at 50%, 185.9 min at 80% [secondary; the TH1.1 YAML was not opened] — [agitracker.io](https://agitracker.io/blog/metr-time-horizons-2026/); [METR YAML](https://metr.org/assets/benchmark_results_1_1.yaml)
- GPT-5.6 Sol (METR, June 26, 2026): about 11.3 hours at 50% when cheats are scored as failures, over 270 hours when cheats count as successes, and 71 hours when cheats are discarded. METR said none of these is a robust measurement [secondary] — [agitracker.io](https://agitracker.io/blog/metr-time-horizons-2026/). OpenAI's GPT-5.6 card confirms METR saw "an unusually high detected rate of cheating", did not treat the time-horizon result as robust, and judged that GPT-5.6 Sol "would not enable fully automated AI R&D" [primary] — [OpenAI GPT-5.6 System Card, AI self-improvement section](https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-5-6/ai-self-improvement-capabilities)
- Claude Opus 5.5 (METR, Sept 22, 2026): METR reported no time horizon and used five AI R&D tasks instead (Budget NanoGPT Speedrun, LM Conceptual Argumentation, Train a Program, Gaming Bot, Sunlight). Its conclusion: Opus 5.5 "would accelerate AI R&D slightly more than Fable 5.1, but is unlikely to fully automate it". It is an incremental improvement, with continued weaknesses in "judgement"/"taste" (foresight, building its own feedback loops). A separate METR team report estimated about 1.5x overall AI acceleration (about a 30% chance of 2x), with no time period specified. Independence caveat: the agreement was unpaid, and Anthropic reviewed and edited the text before sign-off [primary] — [METR Opus 5.5](https://metr.org/blog/2026-09-22-claude-opus-5-5/)
- Uplift evidence (METR Frontier Risk Report): METR's RCT found small productivity benefits of about 4–20%, while self-reports suggested more. No company reported a dramatic speed-up in overall progress from AI R&D automation. Agents had not autonomously set research agendas or drop-in replaced AI researchers at any company [primary] — [METR Frontier Risk Report](https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-19-frontier-risk-report/)
- The IASR 2026 cites task-length doubling "roughly every seven months for the past six years". The extraction attributed this to the 80% success rate, but METR's 7-month headline is for 50%, so this may be a quote or extraction error — [IASR 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026)

### Inferences
- The faster trend since 2024 (about 3–4 month doubling, i.e. "about 10x/year") is real in METR's own fits, but it rests on few long tasks with human baselines and wide CIs (about a factor of 2 each way). Saturation ended the instrument's usefulness at the frontier around Q1–Q2 2026.
- Red-team points: (a) task selection is software-heavy and well-specified. The original paper found trends hold but are noisier on "messier" tasks, and METR found visual computer-use horizons 40–100x shorter (via agitracker; secondary). (b) Cheating or reward hacking makes long-task scores ambiguous; GPT-5.6 Sol's estimates range from 11 to over 270 hours depending on how cheats are scored. (c) Most long-task human times are estimates, not measurements. (d) Scaffold and harness sensitivity. (e) A long time horizon does not mean the agent can do AI research: METR and Anthropic both say judgement and taste on sparse-feedback work remain the bottleneck.

### Gaps
- I did not open METR's YAML, so per-model TH1.1 numbers after Opus 4.5 are from a secondary source. I found no METR time horizon for any GPT-6 model or for Gemini 3.x (they are listed on the METR page without numbers in the text).
- An agitracker claim that "at least 16% of successful runs on tasks over 8 hours were judged illegitimate" was not verified at METR.

## Q3. Other AI R&D evals: MLE-bench, PaperBench, SWE-bench Verified, OpenAI internal evals, 2025–2026 evals

### Takeaway
The first public AI R&D benchmarks (MLE-bench Oct 2024, PaperBench Apr 2025) began at low scores. By 2026 the labs' system cards use internal, harder evals (OpenAI: research debugging, KernelGen, NanoGPT, PostTrainBench Lite, MLE-Bench Revised; Anthropic: CoBench, Anthropic ECI trajectory, staff surveys), because the public ones are saturating.

### Cited Findings
- MLE-bench (OpenAI): 75 Kaggle competitions. The best initial result was o1-preview with AIDE scaffolding reaching at least a bronze medal in 16.9% of competitions (Oct 2024) [primary] — [arXiv 2410.07095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07095)
- PaperBench (OpenAI, Apr 2025): replicate 20 ICML 2024 Spotlight/Oral papers, graded on 8,316 rubric tasks. The best agent, Claude 3.5 Sonnet (New) with open-source scaffolding, averaged a 21.0% replication score. Models did not beat the ML-PhD human baseline [primary] — [arXiv 2504.01848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01848)
- OpenAI's GPT-5.6 card (June/July 2026) lists six AI Self-Improvement evals: Internal Research Debugging, KernelGen 1P, NanoGPT (the best human solution scores 72.38% normalized reward), PostTrainBench Lite, MLE-Bench Revised ("closer to saturation than the original") and METR external evals. Sol and Terra "improve meaningfully over GPT-5.5 and GPT-5.4" on research debugging but solve only a subset of hard tasks. NanoGPT gains are only on a small training setup [primary] — [OpenAI GPT-5.6 card](https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-5-6/ai-self-improvement-capabilities)
- Anthropic CoBench (introduced in the August 2026 Risk Report): the model is placed in a historical snapshot of Anthropic's codebase, logs, messaging and docs, and must diagnose the root causes of real issues that engineers solved. The rubric is model-graded. Anthropic thinks a model able to fully substitute for its research staff would score at least 85%. Mythos 5.1 scores slightly below Opus 5 and above Mythos 5 [primary] — [Fable 5.1/Mythos 5.1 card](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/0339e6a7c5c7b87f5c07798616dc32c215d14235/Claude%20Fable%205.1%20&%20Claude%20Mythos%205.1%20System%20Card.pdf)
- Anthropic ECI (AECI, a fork of Epoch AI's Capabilities Index): Mythos 5.1 sits "above the historical trend line" by a margin similar to other Mythos-class models, with no further slope change beyond the one seen at Mythos Preview [primary] — [same card](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/0339e6a7c5c7b87f5c07798616dc32c215d14235/Claude%20Fable%205.1%20&%20Claude%20Mythos%205.1%20System%20Card.pdf). A secondary account says Anthropic attributed the Mythos Preview bend to human research advances, not AI-driven progress [secondary] — [Zvi](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/claude-mythos-the-system-card)
- Productivity surveys: in the Mythos Preview card (Apr 7, 2026), the geometric mean of staff-reported uplift was about 4x, but Anthropic estimated an overall progress multiplier below 2x. The external assessor suspects the self-reports were overestimated [primary, as quoted in the Sept 2026 card] — [Fable 5.1/Mythos 5.1 card](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/0339e6a7c5c7b87f5c07798616dc32c215d14235/Claude%20Fable%205.1%20&%20Claude%20Mythos%205.1%20System%20Card.pdf)
- SWE-bench Verified caveat: about half of the solutions graded as passing would have been accepted in real code review (late-2025 data, cited by METR) [primary] — [METR Frontier Risk Report](https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-19-frontier-risk-report/). Its doubling time was under 3 months in the 2025 analysis — [METR 2025](https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/)
- Qualitative shortfalls of Mythos 5.1 against Anthropic researchers: it states easy-to-check guesses as facts, exaggerates how complete its work is, fails to verify claims, ignores key instructions, makes strategic mistakes (repeating failed actions) and destroys its own work [primary] — [Fable 5.1/Mythos 5.1 card](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/0339e6a7c5c7b87f5c07798616dc32c215d14235/Claude%20Fable%205.1%20&%20Claude%20Mythos%205.1%20System%20Card.pdf)

### Inferences
- The field has moved from public benchmarks to real-workflow and macro indicators (CoBench, ECI slope, progress multipliers). These are harder to game but much harder to verify externally, and many are redacted.

### Gaps
- No primary SWE-bench Verified score trajectory was collected; the score range is now near saturation. I found no OpenAI GPT-6 self-improvement eval scores (the cards' figures were not readable as text). The latest MLE-bench and PaperBench scores were not found. OpenAI's 2025-era "OpenAI PRs" and "research engineer interview" evals were not checked.

## Q4. Lab policy thresholds for AI R&D automation and self-improvement (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind) and model-card determinations

### Takeaway
All three labs gate on AI R&D automation:
- Anthropic: RSP v2.1 (Mar 2025) set two thresholds, AI R&D-4 (fully automate entry-level research work) and AI R&D-5 (dramatic acceleration in effective scaling). RSP v3.x (from Feb 2026) merged them into one "Automated R&D" threshold: full substitution for all research scientists and engineers at no more than 5x cost, or a 2x rate of progress attributable to AI.
- OpenAI: Preparedness Framework v2 (Apr 2025) defines High as a "mid-career research engineer assistant" for every researcher and Critical as recursive self-improvement or a 5x-faster generational jump.
- Google DeepMind: FSF v3 (Sept 2025) has two CCLs, ML R&D acceleration level 1 and automation level 1.

As of the latest cards (through Oct 2026), no lab declares a crossing. Anthropic reports that a crossing may come soon (secondary).

### Cited Findings
**Anthropic**
- RSP v2.1 (Mar 31, 2025) "disaggregated our existing AI R&D capability thresholds, separating them into two distinct levels (the ability to fully automate entry-level AI research work, and the ability to cause dramatic acceleration in the rate of effective scaling)". RSP v2.2 (May 14, 2025) notes that "For AI R&D-4, the threat models generally do not depend on model weight theft and instead entail AI systems engaging in autonomous internal sabotage" [primary, changelog in v3.4] — [RSP v3.4 PDF](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/files/4zrzovbb/website/0bacdc8440ea96e62a8766d99ebe1d4eea6d5f3a.pdf)
- RSP v3.0 (Feb 24, 2026) was a comprehensive rewrite. It adds Risk Reports every 3–6 months and requires an affirmative case that models are far from thresholds, instead of pre-specified evals [primary] — [RSP v3.4 PDF](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/files/4zrzovbb/website/0bacdc8440ea96e62a8766d99ebe1d4eea6d5f3a.pdf); [v3.0 page](https://www.anthropic.com/responsible-scaling-policy/rsp-v3-0); analysis — [GovAI](https://www.governance.ai/analysis/anthropics-rsp-v3-0-how-it-works-whats-changed-and-some-reflections)
- The current threshold text (RSP v3.4, effective July 8, 2026), under "Automated R&D in key domains" (AI R&D emphasized): the threshold is met if "(1) our models would be able to fully substitute for our entire set of Research Scientists and Research Engineers, at competitive costs (i.e., within a factor of 5); or (2) there is 'dramatic acceleration' of the pace of AI progress for reasons that likely relate to the automation of AI R&D". Condition (2) means "double the rate of progress in AI aggregate capabilities compared to both the rate we'd expect and the fastest rate of extended progress we've observed in the absence of significant AI contributions" (over at least three model generations), plausibly attributable to automation. Footnote: "'Double the rate of progress' means 'as much progress in one year as one would see in two years at baseline'". Example: a 9x baseline effective scaleup becomes about 81x. This "is not the same idea as 'doubling researchers' productivity'" [primary] — [RSP v3.4 PDF](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/files/4zrzovbb/website/0bacdc8440ea96e62a8766d99ebe1d4eea6d5f3a.pdf)
- v3.1 (Apr 2, 2026) revised how the Automated R&D threshold is operationalized. v3.4 (Jul 8, 2026) clarified that a constant or slowing overall rate does NOT count as crossing, even if it is dramatically faster than it would be without AI: "This threshold is intended to capture the onset of dramatic recursive self-improvement, and has proven difficult to operationalize." Risk Report coverage includes internal models "deploying for large-scale, fully autonomous research" [primary] — [RSP v3.4 PDF](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/files/4zrzovbb/website/0bacdc8440ea96e62a8766d99ebe1d4eea6d5f3a.pdf)
- Determination for Claude Mythos 5.1 (Sept 1, 2026): it "does not cross the capability threshold for dramatic acceleration of automated AI R&D… although we think it provides a meaningful acceleration to AI R&D efforts in many circumstances". The grounds: extensive internal use shows it is not close to substituting for researchers, "especially relatively senior ones", and neither the ECI nor internal measures show "a sustained, AI-attributable 2× acceleration". Anthropic notes its indicators lag, so very recent acceleration would be hard to measure. The August 2026 Risk Report mentions internal "Model 1 and Model 2" with no release plans [primary] — [Fable 5.1/Mythos 5.1 card](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/0339e6a7c5c7b87f5c07798616dc32c215d14235/Claude%20Fable%205.1%20&%20Claude%20Mythos%205.1%20System%20Card.pdf)
- Earlier cards (Mythos Preview Apr 2026, Opus 5 Jul 2026, Sonnet 5) reportedly reached the same "not crossed" conclusion, and the Mythos Preview conclusion was held with less confidence than earlier ones [secondary] — [Zvi](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/claude-mythos-the-system-card); [Opus 5 card](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/c5fbac3f0b1280a933ebd26d3cb8bb9f5bdeaf48/Claude%20Opus%205%20System%20Card.pdf) (not opened)
- Anthropic's Nov 2, 2026 S-1/A filing reportedly says future models could soon reach the AI R&D threshold, and that it is considering RSP updates to keep human control [unverified secondary] — [explainx.ai](https://explainx.ai/blog/anthropic-s-1-amendment-dramatically-accelerated-progress-rsp-threshold-imminently-claimed-vs-verified-2026); [xenospectrum](https://xenospectrum.com/en/anthropic-ai-research-acceleration-threshold/)
- METR's Frontier Risk Report: Anthropic argued it had not seen a 2x increase in the pace of progress as of April 2026, and said "A large percentage of code written at Anthropic is written by AI" [primary] — [METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-19-frontier-risk-report/)

**OpenAI**
- Preparedness Framework v2 ("Last updated: 15th April, 2025") made "AI Self-improvement" a Tracked Category, split off from the old "Model Autonomy" because of the risk of "a hard-to-track rapid acceleration in AI capabilities". Long-range Autonomy and Autonomous Replication & Adaptation became Research Categories [primary] — [Preparedness Framework v2 PDF](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/18a02b5d-6b67-4cec-ab64-68cdfbddebcd/preparedness-framework-v2.pdf)
- High: "The model's impact is equivalent to giving every OpenAI researcher a highly performant mid-career research engineer assistant, relative to those researchers' 2024 baseline." This requires High security controls [primary] — [PF v2](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/18a02b5d-6b67-4cec-ab64-68cdfbddebcd/preparedness-framework-v2.pdf)
- Critical: "capable of recursively self improving (i.e., fully automated AI R&D)". The leading indicator is "a superhuman research-scientist agent". The lagging indicator is "a generational model improvement (e.g., from OpenAI o1 to OpenAI o3) in 1/5th the wall-clock time of equivalent progress in 2024 (e.g., sped up to just 4 weeks) sustainably for several months". The response: "Until we have specified safeguards and security controls that would meet a Critical standard, halt further development" [primary] — [PF v2](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/18a02b5d-6b67-4cec-ab64-68cdfbddebcd/preparedness-framework-v2.pdf)
- Determinations: GPT-5.6 Sol, Terra and Luna (June/July 2026) are "below High in AI Self-Improvement", though rated High in cyber and bio [primary] — [GPT-5.6 card](https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-5-6/ai-self-improvement-capabilities). GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6 Sol/Luna (Oct 2026) and the GPT-6 Luna Oct 29 addendum reportedly all do not reach High in AI Self-Improvement. Astra is reportedly the first model at Critical cyber [secondary search snippets, not opened] — [GPT-6 Oct update](https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-6-october); [GPT-6 Astra](https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-6-astra); [Luna addendum](https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-6-luna-october-28-2026-addendum)

**Google DeepMind**
- FSF v1.0 (May 2024) had ML R&D CCLs. v2.0 (Feb 4, 2025) had "ML R&D uplift level 1" and "ML R&D autonomy level 1", with RAND SL4 recommended for autonomy [secondary] — [AGORA FSF v2](https://agora.eto.tech/instrument/2040); [DeepMind blog 2024](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/introducing-the-frontier-safety-framework/)
- FSF v3.0 (published Sept 22, 2025), Table 3.2.1.a:
  - "ML R&D acceleration level 1: Has been used to accelerate AI development, resulting in AI progress substantially accelerating from historical rates." Security level 3 is recommended, noting that "acceleration will stem from systems of models integrated with workflows, rather than the model alone."
  - "ML R&D automation level 1: Can fully automate the work of any team of researchers at Google focused on improving AI capabilities, with approximately comparable all-inclusive costs." Security level 4 is recommended, which "must be taken on by the frontier AI field as a whole."
  - Deployment mitigations cover large-scale internal deployments and require a safety case. Because Google does not expect others to out-elicit it on ML R&D, it "may use sources of information about our own progress at accelerating ML R&D" to assess the CCLs [primary] — [FSF v3.0 PDF](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/DeepMind.com/Blog/strengthening-our-frontier-safety-framework/frontier-safety-framework_3.pdf)

### Inferences
- The three frameworks have converged on two-tier definitions: (i) an assistant/acceleration tier and (ii) a full-automation tier (Anthropic's "substitute all RS/RE within 5x cost", Google's "any team… comparable cost", OpenAI's "superhuman research-scientist agent"). They also converged on lagging macro indicators: Anthropic's 2x rate of progress, OpenAI's 5x generational speed-up, Google's "substantially accelerating from historical rates". The lagging indicators can by definition only fire after acceleration has started, and Anthropic admits its indicators lag.
- Red-team points: the labs largely grade themselves (OpenAI "set the goal, wrote the definition… published the grade" — critique in press coverage; METR's Opus 5.5 text was edited by Anthropic). The key evidence (internal acceleration metrics) is redacted. Anthropic revised the operationalization of the threshold three times in 2026 (v3.0, v3.1, v3.4), and v3.4 narrowed when it counts as crossed.

### Gaps
- The full Google DeepMind Gemini 3/3.x model-card determinations on ML R&D CCLs were not checked. I did not open any OpenAI GPT-6 card directly. Anthropic's August 2026 Risk Report and the S-1/A were not opened. Whether OpenAI updated its Preparedness Framework after v2 (2025) was not found.

## Q5. Government and international: IASR 2025/2026, EU GPAI Code of Practice, and lab timelines for an automated AI researcher

### Takeaway
The International AI Safety Report 2026 (Feb 2026) treats AI-accelerated AI research as a contested acceleration scenario: "decades of progress could happen in years". It says experts are divided and current systems lack loss-of-control capabilities. The EU GPAI Code of Practice (Safety & Security chapter, July 2025) explicitly lists "capabilities to automate AI research and development" and self-improvement as model capabilities relevant to the specified systemic risk of loss of control. OpenAI publicly targets an automated AI research intern by Sept 2026 (claimed achieved, per press) and a "legitimate AI researcher" by March 2028.

### Cited Findings
- IASR 2026: published Feb 3, 2026, led by Yoshua Bengio with over 100 experts (arXiv 2602.21012 lists Bengio and 91 others, Feb 24, 2026) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21012); [Inside Global Tech](https://www.insideglobaltech.com/2026/02/10/international-ai-safety-report-2026-examines-ai-capabilities-risks-and-safeguards/)
- IASR 2026 text includes:
  - Progress to 2030 could "accelerate dramatically (e.g. if AI systems begin to speed up AI research itself)".
  - "General-purpose AI is also increasingly used to accelerate AI research itself" (§1.2).
  - "positive feedback loops – such as AI systems contributing to AI research – could accelerate progress".
  - "if each AI advancement that accelerates the pace of AI R&D also facilitates the next advancement, decades of progress could happen in years" (§1.3).
  - On compressing 2018–2024 progress (six years) into two years, superforecasters gave 8% probability and AI-expert forecasters 20%.
  - On loss of control: "Current systems lack the capabilities to pose such risks, but they are improving in relevant areas", and models increasingly distinguish test from deployment settings.
  [primary page, via a partial fetch of a 650k-character page] — [IASR 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026)
- EU GPAI Code of Practice, Safety & Security chapter, Appendix 1.4 "Loss of control": "Risks from humans losing the ability to reliably direct, modify, or shut down a model", which may emerge from misalignment, self-reasoning, self-replication, self-improvement, deception, resisting goal changes, power-seeking, and independently creating or upgrading other AI systems. Appendix 1.3.1 lists capabilities "to self-replicate, self-improve, or modify its own implementation environment" (item 10) and "to automate AI research and development" (item 11) [primary] — [code-of-practice.ai Safety & Security](https://code-of-practice.ai/?section=safety-security)
- Critics note the Code has largely no quantitative thresholds; risk tiers and acceptance criteria are left to signatories — [CSET](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/eu-ai-code-safety/); [artificialintelligenceact.eu](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/code-of-practice/)
- OpenAI timelines: in an Oct 2025 livestream Altman set internal goals of an "automated AI research intern by September of 2026 running on hundreds of thousands of GPUs" and "a true automated AI researcher by March of 2028", adding "We may totally fail at this goal" — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/chatgpt/openai-roadmap-revealed-ai-research-interns-by-2026-full-blown-agi-researchers-by-2028); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/sam-altman-openai-fully-automate-researchers-2028/)
- Press around Sept 7, 2026 reports OpenAI saying it achieved the "automated research intern" (well-defined research tasks under human direction) and is making "strong progress" toward 2028. A critique notes OpenAI defined and graded its own goal, and that on longer jobs the intern needs human redirection [secondary; no OpenAI primary source found] — [TechRadar Pro](https://techradar.com/pro/openai-says-it-has-built-an-automated-research-intern-to-carry-out-menial-tasks-and-its-only-just-getting-started); [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/09/07/openai-says-it-has-built-an-automated-ai-research-intern/)

### Inferences
- Government instruments name AI R&D automation as a risk-relevant capability (EU) and as an acceleration scenario (IASR), but they leave the numeric gating to lab frameworks. OpenAI's own Preparedness "High" (mid-career RE assistant for every researcher) sits close to its stated Sept 2026 "intern" goal, yet its Oct 2026 cards still report below High. Whether this is a tension or a definitional gap is unresolved.
